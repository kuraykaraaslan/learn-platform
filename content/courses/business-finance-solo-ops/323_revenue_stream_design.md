# 323. Classifying and Balancing Your Revenue Streams

## What It Is
> This lesson is general education, not financial or tax advice. Revenue classification here is a management and pricing tool, not an accounting standard — confirm how your accountant wants these categories mapped to your bookkeeping chart of accounts.

Revenue is not one generic pile of money — different ways of earning it carry very different risk, effort, margin, and predictability, even when the monthly total looks identical. A solo software business typically earns through some mix of fixed-fee projects, time-and-materials work, paid discovery/audits, maintenance retainers, hosting/ops fees, advisory sessions, white-label delivery for agencies, productized services, and occasionally digital products or owned tools. Treating "any paid project is good" as the operating philosophy is how a business ends up busy, cash-positive on paper, and still fragile.

The fix is to classify revenue before judging it. Each stream can be scored on five criteria — predictability, margin, fit with your strengths, control over delivery and payment terms, and growth potential toward larger or repeat work — each rated 1–5. A stream scoring 21–25 should be prioritized; 16–20 is good but needs better structure; 11–15 is acceptable only selectively; anything at 0–10 should be avoided unless there's a clear strategic reason to take it anyway (a portfolio-building loss-leader, for instance).

The target is not to eliminate one-off project work — it's the backbone of most solo software businesses — but to avoid depending on it exclusively. A healthy mix looks something like 50–70% custom project work, 15–30% retainer or maintenance revenue, 10–20% paid discovery or productized offers, and a small experimental slice for anything else. The exact numbers matter less than the habit: before accepting or chasing any piece of work, know which stream it belongs to, whether it's one-time or recurring, and what it costs you operationally beyond the invoice total.

## Key Concepts
- **Revenue stream types**: project_fixed_fee, project_time_materials, paid_discovery, maintenance_retainer, hosting_ops_fee, consulting_session, white_label_delivery, productized_service, digital_product, saas_or_tool — each with a distinct risk profile.
- **Revenue quality score**: rate predictability, margin, fit, control, and growth potential 1–5 each; total 21–25 = prioritize, 16–20 = improve structure, 11–15 = accept selectively, 0–10 = avoid unless strategic.
- **Balanced solo mix**: roughly 50–70% custom projects, 15–30% retainer/maintenance/support, 10–20% paid discovery/productized offers, 0–15% experimental product revenue.
- **Offer-to-stream mapping**: know which stream each of your current offers actually belongs to — an "audit" is paid_discovery, a "monthly improvement package" is maintenance_retainer, and conflating them hides real risk.
- **Required questions per engagement**: which stream is this, is it one-time or recurring, what operational burden does it create, what's the payment schedule, what support obligation remains, and can it become a repeatable offer?
- **Forbidden pattern**: judging revenue only by amount received — a large payment on terrible terms with unlimited support attached can be worse for the business than a smaller, cleaner engagement.

## Example Code
A quarterly revenue-stream breakdown used to spot concentration risk before it becomes a crisis:

```
Q3 2026 REVENUE STREAM REVIEW

Stream                    Revenue    Share    Quality score (1-25)
project_fixed_fee          €18,400     61%    22 (prioritize)
maintenance_retainer        €4,200     14%    19 (improve structure)
paid_discovery               €2,600      9%    17 (improve structure)
project_time_materials       €5,000     16%    11 (accept selectively)

Total                       €30,200    100%

Flag: project_time_materials scored low (unpredictable, capacity-capped,
  one client keeps renegotiating hours down mid-month) — target reducing
  this to <10% of revenue next quarter by converting that client to a
  fixed-scope retainer or letting the relationship lapse.
Target mix check: fixed-fee + retainer + discovery = 84% of revenue,
  comfortably within the healthy 50-70% / 15-30% / 10-20% guideline.
```
The quarterly total looked fine; the stream breakdown is what surfaced the one client quietly eroding margin through open-ended hourly renegotiation.

## When to Use
- Quarterly, as a structured review — alongside or as part of the monthly financial close.
- Before accepting a new type of engagement that doesn't cleanly fit your current offers.
- When revenue feels adequate on paper but the business feels unstable or unusually stressful.
- When deciding which kind of work to actively pursue versus quietly let go.

## Common Mistakes
- **Every paying client is treated as equally good for the business, regardless of terms or support burden** — Treating every paying client as equally good for the business regardless of stream, terms, or support burden.
- **Time-and-materials work keeps growing because it's the easiest thing to say yes to** — Letting time-and-materials work creep upward because it's easy to say yes to, without noticing its low quality score.
- **An engagement is running, and no one's named which revenue stream it actually belongs to** — Never actually naming which revenue stream a given engagement belongs to, which makes the quarterly mix impossible to evaluate.
- **A new SaaS side product gets chased while the core retainer mix is still unstable** — Chasing a new revenue stream (a course, a SaaS side product) while the core project/retainer mix is still unstable.
