# 338. Choosing One Growth Focus Instead of Five

## What It Is
Growth fails for most solo operators not from lack of effort but from scattering effort across cold outreach, LinkedIn content, YouTube, SEO, agency partnerships, a side SaaS idea, a newsletter, and marketplaces all at once. None of these get done well enough to produce a real signal, and every channel gets abandoned right around the point where it might have started working. A solo business has no team to run five bets in parallel — it needs focus.

At any given time, the healthy shape is exactly one primary growth objective, one primary channel, one secondary channel, one core offer being pushed, and one weekly review cadence. Everything else belongs in a backlog sorted into Now, Next, Later, and Not for this year — which protects good ideas from being lost without letting them hijack this month's execution. The objective itself should be chosen deliberately from a short list — more qualified leads, better deal size, more recurring revenue, better positioning, more referrals, more proof, better margins, reduced client concentration, offer productization — based on which one the business actually needs right now, not which one is most exciting.

Committing to a bet also means committing to a minimum trial period before judging it: roughly four weeks for outbound, eight to twelve weeks for content, four to six weeks for referrals, eight to twelve weeks for partnerships, one to two delivery cycles for retainers, two to three sales attempts for a new productized offer, three to five proposals for a pricing change. Judging a content strategy after two posts, or abandoning outbound after one quiet week, is how a solo operator ends up having "tried everything" without ever really testing anything.

## Key Concepts
- **The focus standard**: at any time, exactly one primary growth objective, one primary channel, one secondary channel, one core offer, and one weekly review cadence — everything else is backlog.
- **Objective menu**: more qualified leads, better deal size, more recurring revenue, better positioning, more referrals, more proof, better margins, reduced client concentration, offer productization — pick the one the business actually needs now.
- **Minimum trial periods**: outbound 4 weeks, content 8–12 weeks, referrals 4–6 weeks, partnerships 8–12 weeks, retainers 1–2 delivery cycles, a new productized offer 2–3 sales attempts, a pricing change 3–5 proposals.
- **Growth bet template**: objective, primary ICP, core offer, primary and secondary channel, weekly actions, proof asset needed, success metric, review date, and an explicit stop condition.
- **Backlog discipline**: Now / Next / Later / Not for this year — a place for good ideas that keeps them from becoming this week's distraction.
- **Anti-focus signals to watch for**: changing the offer every week, judging content after two posts, taking poor-fit work because the pipeline is empty, launching a product while delivery is overloaded, building automation before demand exists.

## Example Code
A single growth bet defined with an explicit stop condition, protecting focus for the quarter:

```md
## Growth Bet — Q4 2026

Objective: Convert completed clients into maintenance retainers
  (revenue is currently 90% one-off project work; too unpredictable).
Primary ICP: past clients with a live, stable delivered system.
Core offer: Full Maintenance retainer ($2,200/mo, 5 dev hours included).
Primary channel: direct outreach to past clients (existing trust).
Secondary channel: mention retainer option at every new project's
  handover going forward.
Weekly actions: review 3 old projects, send 2 retainer check-in
  messages, log responses in pipeline.
Proof asset needed: none — relationship trust substitutes for proof
  at this stage.
Success metric: number of retainer conversations booked and signed
  monthly recurring revenue added.
Review date: end of Q4 (12 weeks — matches the 1-2 delivery cycle
  minimum trial period for retainer offers).
Stop condition: fewer than 2 signed retainers after 12 weeks and
  20+ outreach messages -> reassess offer or pricing before repeating.

Backlog (not this quarter): LinkedIn content push, agency partner
  outreach, SEO landing pages -> filed under "Next."
```
Three other reasonable growth ideas got explicitly deferred to the backlog instead of quietly competing for the same limited weekly hours as the one bet actually being executed.

## When to Use
- At the start of any 30–90 day planning period, before committing time to any specific growth activity.
- When feeling scattered across several growth activities with no clear signal from any of them — to force a single choice.
- Before adding a new channel or tactic — to check whether the current bet has actually completed its minimum trial period yet.
- Whenever a compelling new idea appears — to route it into the Now/Next/Later backlog rather than immediately chasing it.

## Common Mistakes
- Running outbound, content, partnerships, and a side product simultaneously, none of them long enough to produce a real signal.
- Abandoning a channel before its minimum trial period has elapsed, based on early impatience rather than evidence.
- Treating every new growth idea as urgent instead of filing it in the backlog for later consideration.
- Chasing a new growth channel while current delivery capacity is already at its limit, guaranteeing that any new leads can't actually be served well.

## Further Reading
- *Essentialism* — Greg McKeown: the broader case for disciplined focus over scattered effort, directly applicable to solo growth planning.
- *Traction* — Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares: a structured approach to testing and committing to growth channels rather than guessing.
- This lesson is general education, not financial or tax advice. Trial-period lengths and objective choices here are directional guidance — adjust to your actual sales cycle and capacity.
