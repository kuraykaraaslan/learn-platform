# 343. Reading Expansion Signals: When to Upsell

## What It Is
> This lesson is general education, not financial or tax advice. Expansion pricing and scope should still go through your standard proposal and contract process before work begins.

An expansion signal is observable evidence that a client's situation has changed in a way that creates real demand for your next offer. Timing matters as much as the offer itself — a well-timed ask on a genuine signal outperforms even the best-crafted pitch delivered at the wrong moment. Calendar-based check-ins at 30, 60, and 90 days create the opportunity to listen, but they should not be the trigger for pitching; the signal is the trigger. Signals fall into five categories: delivery completion (the client asks "what's next?"), usage and adoption ("we're doing this manually still"), business growth (the client is hiring, launching, or entering a new market), relationship health (fast responses, proactive contact, on-time payment), and risk — the negative category that means slow down rather than pitch, such as slow responses, budget contraction language, or a leadership change.

Once a signal is identified, it needs to connect to real value, not just be an opportunity to sell something. Every legitimate upsell ties to one of: more revenue, less manual work, less operational risk, better reporting, better customer experience, higher reliability, better security, or a faster internal workflow. The six expansion categories that map to those values are new modules, automation, integrations, security improvements, performance improvements, and growth features — recommending something outside these categories, or recommending a fix to something that was already in the original scope, is not an upsell, it's either scope creep disguised as a sale or an admission that the original delivery was incomplete.

A simple scoring system keeps this disciplined: a strong Category 1/2 signal is worth +2, a Category 3 business-growth signal is worth +2, strong Category 4 relationship health is worth +2, and having completed the pre-expansion checklist is worth +2 — but any Category 5 risk signal subtracts 4. A score of 6-8 means open the conversation; 3-5 means listen and probe without pitching; 2 or below, or any risk signal present, means resolve the underlying issue first. Acting on a signal always follows the same three steps: acknowledge what was said without immediately pivoting to your offer, ask one clarifying question to confirm the signal is real, and only then propose the next step or explicitly defer.

## Key Concepts
- **Five signal categories**: delivery completion, usage/adoption, business growth, relationship health (all positive triggers), and risk signals (negative — the client is disengaging, budget-constrained, or facing a leadership change, meaning slow down, don't pitch).
- **Signal scoring**: +2 for a Category 1/2 signal, +2 for a Category 3 signal, +2 for strong Category 4 relationship health, +2 for a completed pre-expansion checklist, -4 for any Category 5 risk signal. Score 6-8 = open the conversation; 3-5 = probe only; ≤2 or any -4 = resolve first.
- **Expansion categories mapped to value**: new module, automation, integration, security improvement, performance improvement, growth feature — each must connect to more revenue, less manual work, less risk, better reporting, better CX, higher reliability, better security, or faster workflow.
- **Upsell timing**: good timing is after launch success, after positive feedback, when a manual workaround appears, or before a major campaign; bad timing is before the original scope is delivered, while bugs are unresolved, or when the client is unhappy.
- **Three-step signal response**: acknowledge without pivoting immediately → ask one clarifying question → propose the next step or explicitly defer if the signal turns out weak.
- **Ethical upsell language**: frame the recommendation around the observed business impact, not urgency or fear — "the next highest-value improvement appears to be X, because Y; it's not required, but it would reduce Z."

## Example Code
A worked signal-scoring exercise and the resulting conversation, using a real client comment as the trigger:

```md
## Expansion Signal Assessment — Client E

Observed signals this cycle:
  - Category 2 (usage): "We've had to export this to a spreadsheet
    to share with the ops team a few times this week." → +2
  - Category 3 (business growth): Client mentioned hiring two new
    people last month. → +2
  - Category 4 (relationship health): Replies within 2-3 hours
    consistently; payments on time. → +2
  - Pre-expansion checklist (original delivery stable, no open
    bugs, 60-day review completed): complete. → +2
  - Category 5 (risk): none observed. → +0

Total score: 8 -> STRONG SIGNAL, open the conversation.

Acting on it (live conversation):
  Client: "We ended up exporting the data to a spreadsheet again
    to share with the team."
  You: "How often is that happening now?"
  Client: "A few times a week, especially since we added two more
    people to ops."
  You: "Got it — there's a way to surface that data directly in
    the system so the export step disappears entirely. Want me to
    put together a short outline of what that would look like?"

Mapped to expansion category: Integration / reporting module.
Business impact: removes a manual weekly export step for a team
  that just grew by 40%.
Next step logged in pipeline: outline to be sent within 3 days.
```
The score justified opening the conversation, but the actual ask still waited for one clarifying question before proposing anything.

## When to Use
- During every scheduled account review, as the structured moment to listen for and score signals before deciding whether to pitch.
- The moment a client says something like "we're doing this manually" or "we just hired someone new" — to recognize it as a signal worth probing, not just small talk.
- Before sending any upsell proposal, as a gate: has a real signal been confirmed, and is the signal score high enough to justify the ask?
- Immediately after noticing any Category 5 risk signal — to redirect from expansion planning to relationship recovery instead.

## Common Mistakes
- Pitching an upsell based on the calendar (it's been 60 days) rather than on an actual observed signal.
- Treating a single positive comment as automatic permission to pitch, without asking the clarifying question that would confirm the signal is real and material.
- Recommending an expansion that doesn't map to a genuine business value category — offering a feature because it's interesting to build rather than because it solves an observed problem.
- Ignoring a Category 5 risk signal (slow responses, tightened budget language) and pitching anyway, which reads as tone-deaf and accelerates the relationship's decline.
