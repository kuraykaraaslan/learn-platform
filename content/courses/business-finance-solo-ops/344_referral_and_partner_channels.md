# 344. Building a Referral and Partner Channel

## What It Is
> This lesson is general education, not financial or tax advice. Referral fee arrangements may have tax or disclosure implications depending on your jurisdiction — confirm with your accountant before formalizing a fee-based partnership.

Referrals should not be left to luck. A deliberate referral engine makes it easy for satisfied clients, past colleagues, and adjacent service providers to introduce the right kind of prospect, at the right time, with almost no effort on their part. A good referral ask is specific about the ideal client (not "anyone who needs a developer"), easy to forward as-is, timed after value has clearly been delivered, and respectful of the relationship — never pressuring, never vague. The best moments to ask are when a client confirms satisfaction, a system successfully launches, a support interaction goes well, or a testimonial has just been given; asking during an unresolved issue undermines both the ask and the relationship.

A partner channel is a step beyond a one-off referral ask: it's a recurring relationship with someone who has ongoing access to your ideal client type and is motivated to refer consistently — freelance designers, accountants, digital agencies without in-house dev capacity, or industry-specific advisors. A relationship should not be formalized into a partnership until it has already produced at least one real, qualified referral; formalizing on hope rather than evidence wastes effort on relationships that were never going to be reciprocal. Once qualified, arrangements take one of three shapes: reciprocal referral with no money involved, a referral fee (commonly 5-10% of the first project's value only, paid on contract signature, capped at a defined maximum), or simple cross-promotion to each other's audiences.

Both referral sources and formal partners need light, ongoing maintenance or they go passive — a monthly touchpoint, a quarterly call, and a same-day thank-you after every introduction keep the relationship active. Passive partners (no contact in 90+ days) rarely refer, and a partner sending consistently mis-qualified leads, or one who has sent nothing in twelve months, should be honestly deprioritized rather than kept on indefinitely out of politeness.

## Key Concepts
- **Referral ask standard**: specific about the ideal client, easy to forward verbatim, timed after value is delivered, clear about the next step, and respectful — never pressuring.
- **Referral asset pack**: a one-sentence positioning line, short bio, service/case-study/portfolio links, an intro message the referrer can forward untouched, and an intake link — so the referrer never has to explain your work from scratch.
- **Partner qualification**: don't formalize a partnership until the person has already sent at least one real, qualified referral; confirm audience fit, complementary service, credibility, and reciprocity capacity first.
- **Three arrangement types**: reciprocal referral (no fee, peer-based volume), referral fee (5-10% of first project value only, capped, triggered on contract signature — never on final payment, never applying to renewals), and cross-promotion (mutual audience exposure, no money).
- **Maintenance cadence**: monthly light touch, quarterly call or coffee, same-day thank-you per referral, and an annual check of whether the partner has sent at least two qualified leads that year.
- **Written confirmation, not a legal contract**: a short email restating the fee percentage, cap, trigger, and either party's right to end the arrangement is sufficient before the first referral fee is owed.

## Example Code
A referral ask sent after a successful delivery, followed by the email that formalizes a partner arrangement once a referral has already proven out:

```md
## Referral Ask — Sent After Positive Feedback

Hi Elena,

I'm glad the dashboard project has been useful.

I'm currently looking to work with similar businesses that need
help turning manual reporting or internal workflows into reliable
web platforms and admin systems.

If you know a founder, ops lead, or agency that might need this
kind of technical partner, would you be comfortable introducing us?

You can simply forward this:
"[Your Name] helped us replace three spreadsheets with a live reporting
dashboard. They build production-ready web platforms and workflow
automation. Worth a conversation before scoping any custom project."

---

## Partner Arrangement Email — Sent After One Proven Referral

Hi Marcus,

I appreciated the introduction to Client F — the project went well
and just wrapped up its first milestone.

I work with a few people where we regularly refer clients to each
other when it's a good fit, rather than doing it ad-hoc. Would you
be open to making that more structured?

Proposed terms:
- When you refer a client who signs a project, I'll pay 8% of the
  first project's fee (cap: EUR 800), within 30 days of signature.
  This applies to the first engagement only, not renewals.
- When I refer a client to you, I'd appreciate the same.
- Either of us can end this at any time with notice.

Does this match your understanding? Happy to adjust before we start.
```
The partnership email was only sent after Marcus had already produced one real, qualified referral — not as a speculative pitch to someone who had never sent anyone.

## When to Use
- Immediately after a client confirms satisfaction, a system launches successfully, or a testimonial is received — the highest-trust moments for a referral ask.
- When a referral source has sent a second qualified lead, signaling it's worth formalizing into a defined partner arrangement rather than leaving it ad hoc.
- Quarterly, to review whether each active partner has sent at least the expected volume of qualified leads and whether the relationship still deserves maintenance time.
- When designing outreach materials — to make sure a forwardable one-paragraph description and ideal-client profile exist before asking anyone to refer you.

## Common Mistakes
- Asking for referrals vaguely ("let me know if you hear of anyone") instead of naming the specific ideal client, which produces no forwardable action.
- Formalizing a partner agreement with someone who has never actually sent a real referral, based only on a promising conversation.
- Promising a referral fee verbally and never confirming the percentage, cap, and trigger in writing before the first payment is owed.
- Letting an active partner go quiet for 90+ days and then being surprised when their referral volume drops to zero.
