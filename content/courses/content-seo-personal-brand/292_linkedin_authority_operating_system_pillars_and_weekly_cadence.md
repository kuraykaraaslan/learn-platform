# 292. LinkedIn Authority Operating System: Pillars and Weekly Cadence

## What It Is
LinkedIn authority for a solo technical business runs through seven stages — positioning, profile trust, content pillars, consistent publishing, conversation, soft conversion, and measurement — and every action taken on the platform should serve one of six outcomes: being remembered, trusted, referred, contacted, shortlisted, or invited. That framing matters because it rules out a lot of what LinkedIn "growth" advice actually recommends: maximizing likes, chasing virality, or posting generic motivational content are not on that list, and a post with twelve relevant comments from founders, agency owners, and CTOs is worth more than a viral post that reaches the wrong audience entirely. Authority beats virality specifically because the goal is being trusted by the right dozen people, not liked by the wrong ten thousand.

Content pillars translate that positioning into something repeatable: four to six themes, each tied to a real service, buyer problem, or credibility signal, so posting stops being "whatever crossed my mind today" and becomes a system a stranger can recognize after five posts. A workable default set for a full-stack solo software business is SaaS MVP and product scoping, admin panels and workflow automation, clean full-stack architecture, freelance/project delivery process, UI/UX and product interface thinking, and case studies/build-in-public proof — each with its own purpose (attract founders, attract SMEs, show engineering depth, reduce buyer fear, and so on) and its own example hook. The forbidden pattern here is naming pillars after technologies or vague nouns ("React," "AI," "Business," "Motivation") — these are too broad to actually guide what gets written next; a real pillar should be able to generate at least twenty distinct post ideas without repeating itself.

Cadence turns pillars into an actual habit that survives real client work. The default sustainable rhythm is three posts a week (Monday: problem/point-of-view, Wednesday: technical proof/case study, Friday: process/trust/personal lesson), five to ten meaningful comments per weekday, one proof asset a week, and one relationship-building action per weekday, reviewed weekly. A monthly rhythm should include roughly four problem posts, four technical posts, two case-study/proof posts, two process/trust posts, one soft offer, and one personal story — a mix, not an even split, because problem and technical posts do most of the buyer-attraction and credibility work. Two escape valves matter for a solo operator: a low-energy mode (one post, fifteen comments, one proof screenshot) for busy client weeks that keeps the account from going dark, and a short high-energy launch mode (four to five posts, a carousel, a demo video, heavier commenting and DMs) for two to three weeks around a new service or case study — never sustained indefinitely.

## Key Concepts
- **The seven-stage system**: positioning → profile trust → content pillars → consistent publishing → conversation → soft conversion → measurement.
- **Six outcomes, not virality**: every action should aim to be remembered, trusted, referred, contacted, shortlisted, or invited — likes and impressions are not on this list.
- **Four-to-six pillars tied to real business value**: each pillar must connect to a service, buyer problem, or credibility signal, not a bare technology name.
- **The twenty-idea test for a pillar**: if a pillar can't generate at least twenty distinct post ideas without repeating itself, it's too narrow or too vague.
- **Default weekly cadence**: three posts/week on a Mon/Wed/Fri rhythm (problem, technical proof, process/trust), 5-10 comments/weekday, one proof asset/week.
- **Monthly content mix**: roughly 4 problem, 4 technical, 2 case-study, 2 process, 1 soft offer, 1 personal-story post per month — a deliberately uneven mix.
- **Low-energy and high-energy modes**: a minimal-but-present mode for busy weeks, and a temporary launch-intensity mode for 2-3 weeks around a new offer — never run high-energy mode permanently.
- **Posting-for-the-calendar's-sake anti-pattern**: if a scheduled post has no real insight, turn it into a comment, a draft, or a DM instead of publishing filler.

## Example Code
```md
## Content Pillar Definition

Pillar: Admin Panels and Workflow Automation
Purpose: Attract SMEs with manual, spreadsheet-driven operations
Topics: spreadsheet replacement, approval workflows, dashboards,
        role-based access, reporting, internal tools
Example hook: "If your team needs WhatsApp messages to know the status
of an order, you probably do not need another chat group. You need a
workflow system."

## Default Weekly Schedule

Monday:    1 problem / point-of-view post
Tuesday:   10 relevant comments on target-buyer and peer posts
Wednesday: 1 technical proof or case-study post
Thursday:  relationship-building DMs / replies
Friday:    1 process/trust/personal post + weekly analytics review

## Monthly Content Mix Target
4 problem posts | 4 technical posts | 2 case-study/proof posts
2 process/trust posts | 1 soft offer | 1 personal/founder story
```

## When to Use
- When starting or resetting a LinkedIn presence and posting currently feels random or directionless
- When defining or revisiting the 4-6 pillars a solo technical business should be known for
- When building a content calendar and needing a default cadence that survives a busy client week
- When a busy period is approaching and a sustainable "low-energy mode" needs to be decided in advance rather than defaulting to silence
- Before a new service or case study launch, to decide whether a temporary high-energy posting mode is warranted

## Common Mistakes
- **Content pillars are named "React," "Node.js," "AI"** — Naming pillars after technologies ("React," "Node.js," "AI") instead of buyer value or positioning
- **A post gets huge reach but the people engaging aren't anyone who could hire or refer you** — Chasing viral reach instead of relevant engagement from the specific dozen people who could become clients or referrers
- **A high-energy launch-mode posting cadence is still running three months later** — Running high-energy launch-mode cadence indefinitely instead of for a bounded 2-3 week window
- **A post goes out purely because the calendar says it's due today** — Posting purely to satisfy the calendar with no real insight behind the post
- **One pillar is the fastest to write, so it ends up in most weeks' rotation** — Letting one easy, generic pillar dominate every week because it's the fastest to write
