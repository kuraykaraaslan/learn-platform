# 296. Problem-Solution Posts, Founder Stories, Carousels and Short Video

## What It Is
Beyond the core technical and case-study formats, four other post types round out a sustainable LinkedIn content mix, each solving a different trust or reach problem. **Problem-solution posts** exist specifically to make non-technical buyers feel understood without requiring them to know any technical vocabulary: name a real symptom, explain the root cause underneath it, show what most teams try (and why it's a partial fix), then suggest the better approach and connect it to business impact ("If your team asks 'who has the latest spreadsheet?' every week, the problem is probably not the spreadsheet. The real problem is that your workflow has no single source of truth."). These posts work because they use buyer language — visibility, delays, tracking, reporting — rather than technical terms, and they should never claim custom software is always the answer; ignoring simpler or no-code alternatives when they're genuinely appropriate undermines the credibility the post is trying to build.

**Founder story posts** humanize the person behind the work by connecting a specific personal moment to a professional principle — never oversharing, never generic motivation. The most reliable structural pattern is Before → Realization → Rule ("I used to think a good freelancer was the person who said yes quickly. After a few projects, I learned the opposite. A good freelancer protects the client from unclear scope before writing code."), which always ends by connecting back to a concrete professional practice ("That is why I now define scope before price"). The tone should stay honest, specific, calm, and reflective — over-dramatic hustle-culture framing, client-blaming, and trauma-as-marketing are all forbidden precisely because they trade short-term engagement for long-term credibility.

**Carousels** package a framework, checklist, or educational idea into a short, saveable visual sequence — six to ten slides, one clear idea, never a dense document masquerading as a carousel. The structure (hook/promise → problem → why it happens → framework/checklist → example → mistake to avoid → better approach → summary → CTA) should stay consistent in visual design (cover style, font scale, spacing, author mark) across every carousel so the format itself becomes recognizable. **Short videos** (30-120 seconds, one topic, a clear hook, captioned) come in three flavors — screen demos (problem → screen demo → hidden complexity → takeaway), technical walkthroughs (what I built → why it matters → one technical decision → result), and talking-head insight (hook → point → example → CTA) — and the same confidentiality discipline that governs screenshots applies directly: hide private data, credentials, client names, internal URLs, and tokens before recording, not after.

## Key Concepts
- **Problem-solution structure**: symptom → root cause → what most teams try → better approach → business impact, in buyer language rather than technical vocabulary.
- **Never claim custom software is always the answer**: acknowledging when a simpler or no-code fix is genuinely appropriate builds more credibility than always pitching the bigger solution.
- **Founder story arc**: Before → Realization → Rule, always ending on a concrete professional practice, not a vague inspirational close.
- **Founder story tone discipline**: honest, specific, calm, reflective — never over-dramatic, self-pitying, or client-blaming.
- **Carousel discipline**: 6-10 slides, one idea, consistent visual system (cover, font scale, spacing, author mark, CTA slide) across every carousel published.
- **Carousel structure**: hook → problem → why it happens → framework/checklist → example → mistake to avoid → better approach → summary → CTA.
- **Three short-video formats**: screen demo, technical walkthrough, talking-head insight — each with its own structure but all 30-120 seconds with a clear hook.
- **Screen-recording confidentiality**: hide private data, credentials, client names, internal URLs, and tokens before recording — the same discipline as static screenshots.

## Example Code
```md
## Problem-Solution Post Framework

If <symptom>, the real problem may be <root cause>.
Most teams try <wrong/partial solution>.
But the better approach is <recommended direction>.
This matters because <business impact>.

## Founder Story: Before → Realization → Rule

I used to think <old belief>.
Then <experience> happened.
Now I follow this rule: <principle>.

## Carousel Slide Sequence (6-10 slides)

1. Hook / promise
2. Problem
3. Why it happens
4. Framework or checklist
5. Example
6. Mistake to avoid
7. Better approach
8. Summary
9. CTA

## Short Video Caption Template

A quick look at <topic>.
The visible part is <UI/feature>.
The important part is <technical/business complexity>.
This matters because <buyer impact>.
Takeaway: <one lesson>.
```

## When to Use
- When a post needs to reach non-technical buyers who wouldn't respond to a code-first technical post
- When looking for a lower-pressure, relatable post type to balance a week that's otherwise all technical proof
- When a checklist or framework exists that deserves a saveable, shareable visual format instead of a plain text post
- When a workflow or UI needs to be shown in motion rather than described, and a 30-120 second video would prove it faster than text
- When planning a monthly content mix that needs variety beyond problem posts and technical proof

## Common Mistakes
- Turning every problem-solution post into fear-based selling or insulting the prospect's current process
- Using personal trauma or exaggerated struggle as engagement bait in a founder story
- Overloading carousel slides with full paragraphs instead of one idea per slide
- Recording a screen demo without checking for visible private data, credentials, or client names first
- Publishing carousels or videos with inconsistent visual style so they don't build a recognizable format over time
