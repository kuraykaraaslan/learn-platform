# 297. Comment Strategy, DMs and Networking

## What It Is
Comments and DMs are not filler activity around posting — for a solo technical business, they often generate more business than the posts themselves, because LinkedIn functions as a relationship channel, not a billboard. A comment worth posting adds one of: specific insight, a practical example, a useful question, respectful disagreement, or relevant experience — "Great post!" and "Thanks for sharing!" add nothing and are indistinguishable from spam regardless of intent. Four reusable frameworks cover most good comments: adding a practical layer ("This is especially true when \<scenario\>. I have seen that the hard part is usually not \<obvious thing\>, but \<hidden complexity\>"), asking a smart question, respectfully extending the original point, or offering a mini case example from real experience. Where the time goes matters as much as what gets written: prioritize commenting on founders, agency owners, CTOs, product managers, and SME operators over broad viral posts with no relevant audience, aiming for a roughly 40% target-buyer / 30% peer-technical / 20% partner-agency / 10% high-authority-account split across a minimum of five, ideally ten, meaningful comments per weekday.

This document's scope stops at people you already have a relationship with — existing connections, people who messaged first, warm or referred contacts — deliberately. A connection request to a stranger and the first cold DM after it are a different, higher-obligation motion (contact-data provenance, a legal basis, a suppression check, a shared cross-channel touch budget) that lives in a separate cold-outreach ruleset and is out of scope here. Within warm territory, the rule is simple: a DM must be specific, short, contextual, low-pressure, and not a pitch dump — a comment-to-DM transition should only happen after a real back-and-forth has already established context ("Thanks for the exchange on your post. Your point about \<topic\> was interesting..."), never as an immediate pivot to pitching. An inbound lead deserves a response that gathers context before guessing at a solution ("could you share the business goal, current workflow, must-have features, timeline, and whether there is a budget range already allocated?"), and a warm thread that's gone quiet deserves a low-pressure closing-the-loop message, not repeated unprompted follow-ups.

The forbidden list here protects the relationship value of the whole channel: no spam-generic comments, no hijacking someone else's post with a sales pitch, no arguing aggressively, no long pitch messages immediately after a connection is accepted, no pushing price before context exists, and no guilt-tripping silence. The test for whether a comment is worth posting is simple — could it make a relevant reader think "this person knows what they are talking about"?

## Key Concepts
- **Comments as relationship infrastructure, not filler**: often more valuable to business generation than the original posts they're attached to.
- **What makes a comment worth posting**: specific insight, practical example, useful question, respectful disagreement, or relevant experience — never generic praise.
- **Four comment frameworks**: add a practical layer, ask a smart question, respectfully extend, or offer a mini case example.
- **Where time goes**: prioritize founders, agency owners, CTOs, product managers, and SME operators over broad viral posts with no relevant audience — roughly 40/30/20/10 split across buyer/peer/partner/authority accounts.
- **Warm-only scope**: this discipline covers existing connections and inbound contacts; cold connection requests and first cold DMs are a separate, higher-obligation motion with their own rules.
- **DM standard**: specific, short, contextual, low-pressure, never a pitch dump.
- **Comment-to-DM transition rule**: only move to DM after real back-and-forth exists in the comments — never as an immediate pivot to pitching.
- **Inbound and quiet-thread handling**: gather context before proposing a solution to an inbound lead; close a quiet warm thread gently rather than chasing it repeatedly.

## Example Code
```md
## Comment Frameworks

Practical layer:
"This is especially true when <scenario>. I have seen that the hard
part is usually not <obvious thing>, but <hidden complexity>."

Smart question:
"Curious how you handle <trade-off>? In custom software projects, I
often see this become the point where scope changes."

Mini case example:
"I saw a similar pattern in an admin panel project. The client
originally asked for reports, but the real need was status visibility
across departments."

## Daily Comment Routine

5-10 meaningful comments per weekday
Split: 40% target buyers | 30% peers/technical community
       | 20% partners/agencies | 10% high-authority accounts

## Inbound Lead Response

Thanks for reaching out. Happy to understand this. To avoid guessing,
could you share the business goal, current workflow, must-have
features, timeline, and whether there is a budget range already
allocated?
```

## When to Use
- When building a daily/weekly routine and comments haven't been treated as a deliberate activity with its own quality bar
- When deciding whether an ongoing exchange in someone's comments has earned a move to DM
- When an inbound message arrives and the instinct is to jump straight to a solution or a price
- When a promising warm conversation has gone quiet and a next step is needed that isn't silence or repeated chasing
- When distinguishing which outreach situations are warm (this lesson) versus cold (a separate outreach discipline)

## Common Mistakes
- **A comment just says "Great post!"** — Posting generic praise comments ("Great post!") that add no value and read as spam
- **Comment time goes to a broad viral post whose audience has nothing to do with your target buyer** — Spending comment time under broad viral posts with no relevant target audience
- **A connection request gets accepted, and the very next message is a pitch** — Pivoting to a DM pitch immediately after a connection is accepted, with no established context
- **A DM conversation moves to price before you understand the prospect's actual situation** — Pushing price or a hard pitch before understanding the prospect's actual situation
- **A contact goes quiet, and you send another follow-up, then another** — Sending repeated unprompted follow-ups to a quiet contact instead of one respectful closing-the-loop message
