## What It Is
Making services visible without turning every post into a sales pitch is a real tension, and the resolution is a strict ratio and a specific formula rather than instinct: roughly 80% of content should be pure value, proof, or conversation, 15% soft offer, and 5% direct availability posts — pitching in every post is one of the fastest ways to undo the trust the other 95% built. A soft pitch formula that stays contextual and low-pressure looks like "If you are dealing with \<problem\>, the first step is \<recommended action\>. I help \<target client\> with \<service/outcome\>. You can \<CTA\>" — it names the problem before the offer, which is what keeps it from reading as a cold pitch dropped into an unrelated feed. An occasional direct offer post (best fit / not a fit / next step) is fine at low frequency, but "DM me" under every post, discount-driven CTAs, and publicly pressuring people are all forbidden because they read as desperation, which is the opposite of the authority the whole system is trying to build.

Repurposing exists because a solo operator shouldn't have to invent new content daily — one strong source idea (a client project lesson, a portfolio feature, a technical decision, a common client question) should become multiple LinkedIn assets: a text post, a carousel, a short video, a comment angle, a DM conversation starter, a case-study snippet, and a newsletter/blog teaser. The blog-to-LinkedIn workflow is concrete: extract three strong opinions, one checklist, one technical example, and one buyer-facing problem from a single blog post, and that's three posts and a carousel without writing anything new from scratch. The same logic applies to comments — if a comment sparks real replies or profile visits, that's a signal to expand it into a full post rather than letting the insight disappear into someone else's thread.

Analytics closes the loop, and the discipline here is resisting vanity metrics in favor of quality signals: profile visits, relevant comments, saves, shares, DMs, connection requests from the actual target audience, inbound leads, referrals, and calls booked — not raw impressions or follower count. The signal-interpretation table matters more than any single number: high impressions with low relevant engagement usually means the topic went broad but commercially weak, while low impressions with strong buyer comments usually means the content is working exactly as intended and should continue. A monthly review should ask which pillar produced the most relevant conversations, which post led to a DM or call, and which post attracted the wrong audience — and any experiment (a new hook style, carousel vs. text, a new CTA wording) should be run one at a time, with a working post varied into new angles rather than copied word-for-word.

## Key Concepts
- **The 80/15/5 pitch ratio**: 80% value/proof/conversation, 15% soft offer, 5% direct availability post — pitching in every post erodes the trust built by the rest.
- **Soft pitch formula**: name the problem → state who you help and with what → give a low-pressure CTA, in that order.
- **Repurposing map**: one source idea → text post, carousel, video, comment angle, DM starter, case-study snippet, newsletter teaser.
- **Blog-to-LinkedIn workflow**: extract 3 opinions + 1 checklist + 1 technical example + 1 buyer problem from a single post = 3 posts + 1 carousel.
- **Comment-to-post promotion**: a comment that sparks real replies or profile visits is a signal to expand it into a full post.
- **Quality signals over vanity metrics**: profile visits, relevant comments, saves, shares, DMs, and leads matter; raw impressions and follower count do not on their own.
- **Signal interpretation**: high impressions/low relevant engagement = broad but commercially weak; low impressions/strong buyer comments = working as intended, continue.
- **One experiment at a time**: vary a working post into new angles (different buyer, different example, different format) rather than copying it verbatim or changing everything at once.

## When to Use
- When deciding whether a draft post is pitching too often relative to the 80/15/5 target ratio
- When a completed project or blog post exists and needs to become a week's worth of content instead of one post
- When a comment gets unusual traction and needs to be decided: expand into a post, or let it go
- During the weekly or monthly analytics review, to separate real signal from vanity metrics
- When testing a new format or hook and needing to isolate one variable instead of changing everything at once

## Common Mistakes
- Writing "DM me" or a soft pitch under nearly every post, eroding the trust built by educational content
- Treating one project or blog post as good for exactly one LinkedIn post instead of a full repurposing map
- Chasing impressions and follower count while ignoring DM quality, profile visits, and actual leads
- Copying a high-performing post word-for-word instead of varying it into a genuinely new angle
- Running multiple content experiments simultaneously, making it impossible to tell which change actually worked
