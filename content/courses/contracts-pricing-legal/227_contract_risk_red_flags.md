# 227. Contract Risk Red Flags

## What It Is
Some opportunities cost more than they pay, and the warning signs are usually visible before any work starts — the hard part is not the diagnosis, it's acting on it while a prospect is still enthusiastic and the freelancer is worried about losing the deal. A short, memorized list of critical red flags — no deposit, payment only after full launch, unlimited revisions, scope to be "defined during development," no written agreement, no single decision maker, source code released before final payment, a large unclear scope paired with an urgent deadline — turns a vague uneasy feeling into a concrete, nameable risk that can be addressed directly instead of rationalized away.

Each red flag has a calm, professional, non-adversarial response that corrects the term without treating the prospect as an adversary. "For custom software projects, I start after written scope approval and an initial deposit — that protects scheduling and ensures both sides are committed" says the same thing as "I don't trust you enough to start without money down," but only one of those framings actually gets said, and it's the one that preserves the relationship while still protecting the freelancer. The same logic applies to unlimited revisions, payment-after-launch, and vague scope: each has a scripted, matter-of-fact correction that redirects the prospect toward a workable structure instead of either accepting the risk or losing the deal by refusing outright.

A severity framework helps prioritize which flags are non-negotiable and which are manageable with an added clause: no deposit, unlimited revisions, vague scope, and payment-only-after-launch sit at the critical tier and generally warrant declining if the client won't correct them; no decision maker, refusal of exclusions, and demands for a legal compliance guarantee sit at high severity and need a firm response before proceeding; an unclear third-party dependency is a medium-severity item that can usually be handled by adding an explicit assumption clause rather than walking away. This lesson is about recognizing commercial risk before it becomes a legal or financial problem — it isn't a substitute for a lawyer's review of any contract you're actually about to sign, particularly one where a client has proposed unusual or aggressive terms.

## Key Concepts
- **Critical red flags**: no deposit, payment only after full launch, unlimited revisions, scope to be decided during development, no written agreement, no single decision maker, source code before final payment, and a large unclear scope paired with an urgent deadline.
- **Severity tiers**: critical (require correction or decline), high (require a firm response before proceeding), and medium (usually resolved with an added assumption or dependency clause rather than walking away).
- **Protective response scripts**: pre-written, calm, non-adversarial language for each common red flag, so the moment of pushback doesn't have to be improvised under pressure.
- **Vague-scope response**: redirecting to a paid discovery phase or a smaller, clearly defined MVP rather than quoting a fixed number against an undefined idea.
- **Decline-as-a-valid-outcome principle**: some opportunities should be turned down rather than accepted with unresolved critical risk, and recognizing that early costs far less than discovering it mid-project.

## Example Code
```template
## Red Flag Response Scripts

**No deposit:**
"For custom software projects, I start after written scope approval and
an initial deposit — that protects scheduling and ensures both sides are
committed."

**Payment after launch only:**
"I don't work on a full payment-after-launch model. I use milestone-based
payments tied to visible progress and delivery checkpoints."

**Unlimited revisions requested:**
"I include defined revision rounds so feedback stays manageable. Unlimited
revisions make timeline and scope impossible to plan around."

**Vague scope, fixed-price request:**
"The idea isn't specific enough yet for a fixed estimate. The right next
step is a paid discovery phase or a smaller, clearly defined first version."

## Risk Severity Quick Reference
| Red Flag | Severity | Response |
|---|---:|---|
| No deposit | Critical | Require deposit or decline |
| Unlimited revisions | Critical | Replace with a bounded revision policy |
| Vague scope | Critical | Paid discovery or smaller defined MVP |
| No decision maker | High | Require one before discovery/SOW |
| Unclear third-party dependency | Medium | Add explicit assumption/dependency clause |
```

## When to Use
- Before accepting any new project, as a standard pre-commitment check.
- During a sales conversation, the moment any of the critical phrases ("we'll figure out features as we go," "pay you after launch") appears.
- When deciding whether an unusual client demand needs a firm correction or is actually manageable with an added clause.

## Common Mistakes
- **No deposit and a vague scope both show up in the first call, and the deal still feels too good to walk away from** — Recognizing a red flag internally but rationalizing it away because the deal otherwise looks attractive.
- **Asking for a deposit comes out sounding like an apology instead of a normal, matter-of-fact term** — Responding to a red flag with an apologetic or defensive tone instead of a calm, matter-of-fact correction.
- **Energy goes into negotiating a minor third-party dependency clause while the no-deposit red flag sails through unaddressed** — Treating every red flag as equally severe, spending scarce negotiating energy on a medium-severity item while accepting a critical one.
- **A dispute is already underway before anyone notices that three red flags were visible in the very first conversation** — Waiting until a dispute is already underway to notice that several red flags were present from the very first conversation.
